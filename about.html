Authorship

The Awareness Theory of Everything (ATE) is developed by Nathan Hamaoui as an independent foundational framework for physics, emergence, and reality.

A Different Starting Point

Most physical theories begin with matter, energy, or spacetime as their fundamental ingredients. The Awareness Theory of Everything (ATE) begins somewhere even more basic:

That something exists at all — and that existence is inseparable from awareness.

ATE does not treat awareness as a byproduct of complex brains, nor as a mystical substance. Instead, awareness is taken in a strictly minimal, structural sense: the capacity for distinction, recognition, or classification.

What Does “Awareness” Mean Here?

In ATE, awareness does not mean consciousness, thought, emotion, or self-reflection. Those are higher-level emergent phenomena.

At its most primitive level, awareness simply means that distinctions can occur at all — that something can be this rather than that. From this minimal capability, increasingly complex structures can be built.

The central claim of ATE is that once such minimal awareness exists, a recursive process of classification and stabilization naturally unfolds, giving rise to spacetime, particles, forces, chemistry, biology, and beyond.

Why Take This Approach?

Modern physics has achieved extraordinary predictive success, yet it rests on a patchwork of foundational assumptions: spacetime here, quantum fields there, initial conditions inserted by hand.

ATE explores whether a single structural principle — recursive awareness — can underlie all of these without requiring separate postulates for each domain.

The goal is not to replace existing physics, but to explain why its structures exist at all.

What ATE Is — and Is Not

ATE is:

  • a structural framework rather than a speculative philosophy
  • compatible with empirical science and falsifiability
  • intended to unify physics, life, and cognition under a common logic

ATE is not:

  • a spiritual or religious doctrine
  • a denial of existing physical theories
  • a claim that human consciousness creates reality
